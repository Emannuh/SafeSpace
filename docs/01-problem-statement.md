# Problem Statement

## Background

Access to legal rights and protection information can significantly influence how people respond during difficult or high-risk situations.

In Kenya, young people may encounter situations involving teenage pregnancy, sexual exploitation, abuse, police interaction, arrest, or other justice-related processes without clearly understanding the rights and protections available to them.

While legal and institutional information exists, it is often fragmented across legislation, government agencies, policies, court processes, and public institutions.

The language used may also be difficult for young people to understand.

## Core Problem

Young people in vulnerable situations may not know:

- whether what is happening to them is lawful
- what rights they have
- what legal procedures should be followed
- which institution is responsible
- where they can safely seek assistance
- what they should do next

This creates an information asymmetry between the individual and the institution, authority, or person they are interacting with.

## Information Access Gap

The issue is not always that information does not exist.

The problem is that the information may be:

- difficult to find
- difficult to understand
- outdated
- fragmented
- inaccessible during urgent situations
- disconnected from clear next actions

## Proposed Response

SafeSpace aims to provide young people with simple, traceable, and actionable rights information.

The system will not attempt to replace lawyers, courts, police, counsellors, or emergency services.

Instead, SafeSpace will help users:

Understand:
What rights or protections may apply?

Verify:
Where does the information come from?

Act:
What can the user safely do next?

Protect:
What support service or institution may be appropriate?
